# Nexli Funding Objection Handling Guide

## Introduction

Objections are a natural part of the sales process and often indicate genuine interest. This guide provides proven responses to the most common objections encountered in each workflow stage.

**The HEAR Method**:
- **H**alt - Stop talking and listen completely
- **E**mpathize - Acknowledge their concern
- **A**nswer - Provide a clear, factual response
- **R**econfirm - Ask for commitment to move forward

---

## Universal Objection Principles

### 1. Never Argue with the Prospect
Instead of "That's not true," say "I understand why you might feel that way..."

### 2. Use the Feel, Felt, Found Technique
"I understand how you feel. Many business owners have felt the same way. What we've found is..."

### 3. Ask Questions to Understand the Root Concern
"Help me understand what specifically concerns you about..."

### 4. Use Social Proof When Appropriate
"Just last week, I helped a business owner in a similar situation..."

## WF-1: Initial Contact Objections (NEW → WORKING)

### "I'm too busy right now"

**Response:**
"I completely understand - running a business keeps you incredibly busy. That's exactly why I'm calling right now while your application is hot. 

The document upload takes just 5 minutes, and I can walk you through it step by step. If we wait, you'll have to start the whole application process over again.

Can you spare 5 minutes right now to secure your funding, or would you prefer to call me back in the next hour before your pre-qualification expires?"

**Follow-up if still resistant:**
"I hear you. Let me ask you this - is the funding still important for your business? [Wait for yes] Great. When specifically can you give me 5 minutes today?"

---

### "I need to think about it"

**Response:**
"That makes perfect sense - this is an important decision for your business. What specific aspects do you need to think about?

[Listen to their response]

I can help you think through those concerns right now. The pre-qualification you received is time-sensitive, and I'd hate for you to lose this opportunity while you're thinking about it.

What questions can I answer that would help you make a decision today?"

**Alternative approach:**
"I understand. In my experience, when business owners say they need to think about it, they usually have a specific concern. What's your biggest concern about moving forward?"

---

### "I want to shop around for better rates"

**Response:**
"That's smart business thinking - you should always compare options. Here's the thing though: this pre-qualification is based on your specific situation right now, and it expires if we don't move forward.

Most business owners find that shopping around takes weeks, and by then, their original qualification is gone and they have to start over.

Let me ask you this - if I could guarantee these are competitive rates for your situation, would you be ready to move forward today?"

**Follow-up:**
"I work with multiple lenders every day, and these rates are already the result of shopping your application around. That's how we got you the best terms available for your profile."

---

### "I don't have the documents ready"

**Response:**
"No problem at all! That's actually very common. The good news is that most of the documents we need are things you probably have on your phone or computer already.

Let me walk you through exactly what we need, and I bet you have most of it available right now:

[List the specific documents]

Do you have access to your bank account online? Perfect - we can get your statements in about 2 minutes..."

**For missing documents:**
"For anything you don't have immediately available, I can give you 24 hours to get it uploaded. The important thing is we get started today so you don't lose your pre-qualification."

---

### "I've been burned by funding companies before"

**Response:**
"I'm really sorry to hear that, and unfortunately, your experience isn't unique. There are a lot of companies out there that don't operate ethically.

That's exactly why I want to be completely transparent with you about our process. Here's exactly what happens next: [detailed explanation]

Unlike other companies, we don't charge any upfront fees, and there are no surprises. You see all terms before you sign anything.

What specifically happened with the other company? I want to make sure we don't repeat any of those issues."

**Social proof:**
"I work with business owners every day who had bad experiences elsewhere. The difference is we've funded over [number] businesses, and our process is completely transparent from start to finish."

---

## WF-2: Document Chase Objections (WORKING → DECISION)

### "I'm having trouble getting the documents"

**Response:**
"I understand - sometimes getting all the paperwork together can be challenging. Let me help you solve this step by step.

Which specific documents are giving you trouble?

[Listen to their specific challenges]

Here's what we can do: [provide specific solutions for each document type]

I can also give you a 24-hour extension, but after that, your pre-qualification expires and we'd have to start over. When can you have these uploaded?"

---

### "I don't feel comfortable uploading financial documents online"

**Response:**
"Your concern about security is completely valid - your financial information should be protected. Let me explain our security measures:

Our portal uses bank-level encryption, the same security that protects online banking. The documents go directly to our secure servers and are only accessed by our underwriting team.

We've processed thousands of applications this way with zero security issues. However, if you're still uncomfortable, I can arrange for you to email them directly to me or fax them to our secure line.

What would make you feel most comfortable?"

---

### "The bank statements are too old/don't show enough revenue"

**Response:**
"I appreciate you being upfront about that. Here's the good news - our lenders understand that business revenue fluctuates, especially recently.

What matters more is the overall trend and your ability to repay. Can you tell me about your typical monthly revenue?

[Listen to their explanation]

Based on what you're telling me, you might actually qualify for a different product that's more suitable for your situation. Let me check what other options we have available for you."

**Alternative:**
"Do you have more recent statements that show better numbers? Sometimes we can work with 30-60 day old statements if they better represent your business."

---

### "I changed my mind about the funding amount"

**Response:**
"No problem at all - it's important that the funding amount fits your needs. What amount would work better for your situation?

[Listen to their response]

The good news is that most of our products are flexible. If you want a smaller amount, that usually means better terms and faster approval. If you need more, we might have different products available.

Let me see what options we have for [new amount]. The important thing is we complete your current application so we don't lose your qualification, and then I can present you with the options that fit your new requirements."

## WF-4: Offer Review Objections (DECISION → FUNDED)

### "The rate is too high"

**Response:**
"I understand your concern about the rate. Let me put this in perspective for you.

This rate is based on several factors: [explain factors like credit, revenue, industry, etc.]

For your specific situation, this is actually competitive. Let me ask you this - what rate were you expecting?

[Listen to their response]

Here's what I want you to consider: the cost of NOT having this funding. How much revenue could you generate with this capital? How much money are you losing every day without it?

Most of our clients find that the revenue generated from the funding far outweighs the cost."

**Alternative approach:**
"What if I could show you exactly how this investment pays for itself? [Do the math with them showing ROI]"

---

### "I need to discuss this with my business partner/spouse"

**Response:**
"Absolutely - that's a smart business practice. How long do you think that conversation will take?

Here's my concern: this offer expires at [time] today, and the lender won't extend it. If we miss this deadline, you'll have to reapply and might not get the same terms.

Is your partner available right now? I'd be happy to conference them in so we can all discuss this together. Or would you prefer to call them right now and have the conversation while I wait?"

**If they insist on waiting:**
"I understand. Let me see if I can get the lender to extend the deadline until tomorrow morning. I can't promise anything, but let me try. Can you commit to giving me an answer first thing tomorrow if I can make that happen?"

---

### "I want to think about it overnight"

**Response:**
"I completely respect that - this is an important decision. Let me ask you this: what specific aspects do you want to think about?

[Listen to their concerns and address each one]

I understand the desire to sleep on it, but here's the situation: the lender requires a decision today because they have limited funding available at these rates.

If you're generally happy with the terms but just want time to process, I can hold the line for 10 minutes while you take a quick break to think it through. What do you say?"

**Alternative:**
"Help me understand - is there something about the terms that concerns you, or do you just prefer to think about big decisions overnight? If it's concerns about the terms, let's address those right now."

---

### "I need to see if I can get better terms elsewhere"

**Response:**
"I understand that completely - you'd be foolish not to compare options for your business.

Here's what I need you to know: I've already shopped your application to multiple lenders to get you these terms. This IS the result of shopping around.

If you want to apply elsewhere, you certainly can, but you'll start the entire process over - new applications, new document requirements, new underwriting. That typically takes 2-3 weeks.

Meanwhile, this offer expires today, and you'll lose the certainty of having funding approved and ready.

Is the potential for slightly better terms worth the risk of losing this guaranteed approval and delaying your funding by weeks?"

---

### "I can't afford the payments"

**Response:**
"Let's talk about that. You originally applied for [amount] for [purpose]. Has your business situation changed since then?

[Listen to their response]

Help me understand your cash flow. What payment amount would work comfortably for your business?

[Listen to their response]

Let me see if we have any flexibility. I might be able to structure this differently or find a different product that better fits your cash flow. 

The alternative might be a smaller amount with lower payments. Would you rather have [smaller amount] with payments you're comfortable with, or wait and risk losing the opportunity altogether?"

---

### "I found a better deal elsewhere"

**Response:**
"That's great! I'm glad you found something that works. Before you make a final decision, can you share the terms you found? I want to make sure you're comparing apples to apples.

[Listen to their 'better' offer]

[If their comparison is fair]:
'You're right, that is a better deal. I'm disappointed we couldn't match it, but I'm glad you found something that works for your business.'

[If their comparison isn't fair]:
'Let me make sure I understand correctly... [clarify their terms]. That does sound good. Just make sure you understand [specific concerns about their deal]. Sometimes what sounds better upfront can cost more in the long run.'

[If they seem unsure]:
'How confident are you that this other option will actually fund? The advantage of our offer is that it's guaranteed and you can have the money tomorrow.'"

## WF-6: Nurture Re-engagement Objections

### "I'm no longer interested in funding"

**Response:**
"I understand - business needs change. Can I ask what changed? Did you find funding elsewhere, or did your business situation improve?

[Listen to their response]

That's great to hear! Even though you don't need funding right now, I'd love to stay in touch. Business needs can change quickly, and we're always here if something comes up.

Also, do you know any other business owners who might be looking for funding? We have a referral program that [explain benefits]."

---

### "I had a bad experience with your company before"

**Response:**
"I'm really sorry to hear that, and I take full responsibility for whatever happened. That's definitely not the experience we want any business owner to have.

Can you tell me specifically what went wrong? I'd like to understand so I can make sure it doesn't happen again.

[Listen carefully and acknowledge their experience]

I can't change what happened before, but I can tell you that things have changed significantly since then. [Explain improvements]

Would you be willing to give us another chance to earn your business? I'll personally make sure you have a completely different experience this time."

---

### "I'm working with another funding company"

**Response:**
"That's fine - it's smart to explore your options. How's that process going for you?

[Listen to their response]

I'm not here to interfere with that process. I'm just calling because we have some new programs that might be worth knowing about for the future.

Even if you're happy with your current situation, it never hurts to have a backup plan. Can I take just 2 minutes to tell you about what's available? That way you'll know your options if anything changes."

## General Objection Handling Strategies

### The "Takeaway" Technique
When a prospect seems resistant, sometimes removing pressure helps:

"You know what, [Name]? It sounds like this might not be the right time for you. That's perfectly fine - I'd rather work with business owners who are excited about the opportunity.

Why don't I close your file for now, and if your situation changes in the future, feel free to give me a call."

*Wait for their response - often they'll re-engage*

### The "Boomerang" Technique
Turn their objection into a reason to move forward:

"The fact that you're concerned about [objection] tells me you're exactly the kind of careful business owner we want to work with. That's why this program is perfect for you..."

### The "Isolation" Technique
Identify if this is their only concern:

"I understand your concern about [objection]. If I could address that issue to your satisfaction, is there anything else that would prevent you from moving forward today?"

### The "Clarification" Technique
Make sure you understand the real objection:

"Help me understand... when you say [objection], do you mean [clarification]? Or is it more about [alternative]?"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Don't:
- Argue or get defensive
- Use high-pressure tactics
- Make promises you can't keep
- Talk over the prospect
- Take objections personally
- Give up after the first objection

### Do:
- Listen completely before responding
- Acknowledge their concerns
- Ask clarifying questions
- Use evidence and social proof
- Stay calm and professional
- Be willing to walk away if it's not a fit

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Multiple Objections
Prospect: "I'm too busy, I need to think about it, and I want to shop around."

**Approach**: Address one objection at a time, starting with the most serious. Ask which is their biggest concern.

### Scenario 2: The Silent Treatment
Prospect becomes quiet after your presentation.

**Approach**: "I can tell you're thinking this through. What questions are going through your mind right now?"

### Scenario 3: The Aggressive Prospect
Prospect becomes hostile or accusatory.

**Approach**: Stay calm, acknowledge their feelings, and try to understand the root cause. If they remain abusive, politely end the call.

### Scenario 4: The Indecisive Prospect
Prospect agrees with everything but won't commit.

**Approach**: Create urgency, ask for the sale directly, or use the isolation technique to find their real concern.

---

**Remember**: The goal of objection handling isn't to overcome every objection, but to have honest conversations that lead to mutually beneficial outcomes. Some prospects aren't a good fit, and that's okay.
